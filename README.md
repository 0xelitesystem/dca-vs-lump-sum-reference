# DCA vs Lump Sum

A plain-language reference on dollar-cost averaging versus investing a lump sum: what each one is, what the evidence actually says about returns, why the lower-regret choice is often not the higher-return one, and how to decide based on your own situation rather than a headline.

**Not financial advice.** This explains two approaches and the tradeoffs between them. It does not tell you what to buy, when, or how much. Markets carry risk, past patterns do not predict the future, and your situation is yours. Take it with a grain of salt and do your own due diligence.

## The core idea

Lump sum means investing all the money at once. Dollar-cost averaging means splitting it into equal amounts invested on a fixed schedule. On average, across long histories, investing the lump sum sooner has tended to end ahead, because markets rise more often than they fall. Dollar-cost averaging trades some of that expected return for lower regret if the market drops right after you invest.

## The honest framing

There is no single right answer, and anyone who says otherwise is selling certainty that markets do not offer. The average case and your own risk tolerance often point in different directions. This reference lays out both so you can choose with eyes open.
