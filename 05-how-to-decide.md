# How to decide

Decide by asking whether you hold a lump sum now, how long your horizon is, and how you would react to a sharp loss soon after investing. The honest answer to the last question usually settles it.

## A short decision path

First, do you actually have a lump sum to invest now, or are you investing income as it arrives? If income as it arrives, you are already dollar-cost averaging and there is no decision to make. If you hold a lump, continue.

Second, what is your time horizon? A longer horizon strengthens the case for getting fully invested sooner, because more time means more of the average upward drift.

Third, and most important, how would you react if the market dropped sharply right after you invested the full amount? If you would hold steady, the average case favors lump sum. If you might sell, the behavioral protection of DCA is worth its cost.

## A middle path

If you cannot decide, a partial approach exists: invest a portion as a lump sum now and dollar-cost average the rest over a set period. This splits the difference, taking some of the average-return advantage of lump sum while keeping some of the regret protection of DCA. It is a reasonable compromise for someone genuinely torn.

## The disclaimer that belongs here

This is a framework for thinking, not a recommendation. The right choice depends on your finances, your goals, and your temperament, none of which a reference can know. For a decision that carries real weight, the responsible step is to think it through against your own situation and, if useful, talk to a qualified professional. Take all of it with a grain of salt.
