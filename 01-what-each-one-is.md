# What each one is

Lump sum investing puts all available money into the market at once. Dollar-cost averaging, or DCA, splits the same money into equal parts and invests them on a fixed schedule regardless of price. Both deploy the same total; they differ only in timing.

## Lump sum

A lump sum invests the full amount on day one. If you have a sum to invest, whether from savings, a windfall, or a sale, lump sum means it all goes in now. The money is exposed to the market immediately and fully, which is the source of both its higher average return and its higher short-term risk.

## Dollar-cost averaging

Dollar-cost averaging divides the amount into equal installments invested at regular intervals, such as the same dollar amount each month. Because you buy at many prices over time, you avoid putting everything in at a single price, and you buy more units when prices are lower and fewer when they are higher. The money enters the market gradually rather than all at once.

## A simple example

Suppose you have a fixed amount to invest. Lump sum puts it all in today at today's price. DCA might invest one twelfth of it each month for a year. If the market rises steadily over that year, lump sum wins, because more of your money was invested earlier at lower prices. If the market falls and then recovers, DCA can come out ahead, because the installments bought in at the lower prices along the way.

## The same money, a different bet

The key point is that DCA and lump sum are not different investments; they are different timing choices for the same investment. The choice is really about how much short-term timing risk you want to take on, not about what you are buying. That framing makes the tradeoff in the rest of this reference clearer.
